# Atlas — Marketing Strategy (one page)

**Status:** decision skeleton. Each section has a **recommended default** — confirm or override. This is the layer the landing relaunch plan should serve; once settled, we revise the plan to match, then build.

---

## 1. Audiences & priority (decided with Aris)

**Primary buyer — B2B: gyms, studios, personal trainers.** They pay (eventually); they're who the landing's hero and the founding-partner offer speak to. **Goal: founding-partner applications.**

**Secondary — B2C traffic: solo athletes.** Not the buyer. A top-of-funnel engine: free app installs → (a) social proof for the B2B pitch, (b) app-store ranking, (c) a member base that makes studios stickier and can pull their gym in. **Goal: installs.**

> The discipline: **one primary.** The page, the spend, and the metrics optimise for B2B applications. B2C is a deliberate awareness/proof engine, not a competing goal — never let it dilute the studio pitch.

---

## 2. Positioning (recommended default — confirm)

**"The gym platform your members actually want to open."**

The wedge: most gym software (Mindbody, TeamUp, Glofox, etc.) is admin-first — booking + payments for the *owner*, and a member app members tolerate. Atlas flips it: it runs the studio's classes/bookings/passes **and** ships every member a genuinely good free training app — a 1,900-exercise video library, offline-first workout tracking, programs, challenges. Better member engagement is the differentiator, and it's *also* what makes a studio stick (engaged members don't churn).

- **To studios:** "Run your classes, and give your members an app they'll actually use — not another admin portal they ignore."
- **One-liner test:** competitors sell the owner a back-office; Atlas sells the owner *member engagement*.

*(Alternative positions if you disagree: "the all-in-one for modern studios" (generic, weaker) or "the free training app that also runs your gym" (leads B2C — wrong primary). Recommend the member-engagement wedge.)*

---

## 3. The two funnels

| | B2B (primary) | B2C (traffic) |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Studio owners, PTs, physio | Solo athletes / fitness consumers |
| Message | Booking + passes + members love the app | Free app, 1,900 video exercises, offline tracker |
| Destination | Landing → **founding-partner application** | Ad → **App Store install** (or /download) |
| Primary metric | Applications/week → pilots signed | Installs, cost-per-install, D7 retention |
| Landing role | Hero + form speak to studios | Secondary "free tracker" story only |

## 4. How to advertise (TikTok / Instagram / Facebook)

**The key leverage point: your exercise-demo video library is native short-form content.** You have ~1,869 pro exercise clips in R2 — that's exactly what performs on fitness TikTok/IG. The B2C engine can be fed almost for free by repurposing them. Don't sleep on this.

**B2C engine (installs) — where paid social actually shines:**
- Creative: exercise-demo clips (the R2 assets), "free app · 1,900 video exercises · offline," quick tracker/logging clips. Native, high-volume, cheap.
- Targeting: broad fitness-consumer interests on TikTok/IG/FB; let the platform optimise for installs.
- Mix: organic posting (a demo-clip-a-day cadence costs nothing) + modest paid install campaigns. Organic first.

**B2B (applications) — be honest that paid social is a blunt tool here:**
- Studio owners/PTs are a *niche* audience; precise B2B targeting on TikTok/IG/FB is inefficient and CAC runs high. Reserve most paid budget for the B2C engine.
- What actually works B2B at your stage: **founder-led content** (post the build + the product as the person who made it — the "engineer who listened to gym owners" story is strong and authentic), **direct outreach** to local studios/PTs, and **the pilot as proof/referral**. Meta interest-targeting (gym owners + small-business) for light retargeting of landing visitors is fine; don't lead with cold paid B2B.
- Landing does the converting: studio-focused hero + the founding-partner form.

**The reinforcement loop:** B2C installs → "10k+ athletes train on Atlas" social proof → strengthens the B2B pitch. Studios onboard → their members install → more B2C numbers. Real, but keep the goals separate in measurement.

**Recommended budget posture (early/pilot):** paid → mostly the B2C install engine (cheap, builds proof); B2B → founder content + outreach + pilot case study (not cold paid). Revisit once you have a repeatable application→pilot conversion.

---

## 5. Leverage the pilot studio (new, decisive)

You have a real onboarded studio with classes going live this week. Use it:
- **Proof on the landing:** "Now onboarding studios like [name]" / a short testimonial / logo. Replaces founder-quote-only credibility with a real customer — the single biggest trust upgrade available.
- **Validate positioning:** ask them directly whether "runs classes + members love the app" is why they said yes, or whether they only cared about booking. That answer should shape the hero. (This is the real-buyer lens no panel can fake.)
- **Real footage:** film the booking demo videos against their real schedule/classes/bookings — authentic beats mocked.
- **Case study:** capture before/after (spreadsheets/WhatsApp → Atlas; no-shows, admin time) — the core B2B ad + landing narrative.

---

## 6. Success metrics (define before spend)

- **B2B (primary):** founding-partner applications/week; application → pilot conversion; time-to-first-published-class; pilot retention/engagement.
- **B2C (secondary):** installs; cost-per-install; D1/D7 retention (proof they're *real* users, not vanity installs).
- **Landing:** application-form conversion rate; ad→landing message-match (bounce).
- A relaunch is "successful" only against a number — set the target (e.g. *X applications/week at ≤£Y blended CAC*) before building.

---

## 7. How this revises the relaunch plan

- **Primacy:** the landing hero + primary CTA speak to **studios** (application), not a 50/50 split. The free-tracker/member story stays but as the *secondary* journey, not the headline.
- **The exercise-library showcase earns double duty:** proof for B2B on the landing *and* the content well for the B2C ad engine — build it once, use it twice.
- **Booking is the B2B hook** and leads the studio story (matches the pillar we found missing).
- Pilot proof replaces the placeholder credibility.

---

## 8. Decisions (confirmed with Aris)

1. **Positioning** — CONFIRMED: the member-engagement wedge (§2).
2. **Primary CTA** — CONFIRMED: founding-partner application is *the* goal; store badges secondary.
3. **B2C spend** — organic-first now; a small paid budget (~€100/mo) planned but ~3–4 months out, not yet.
4. **Pilot visibility** — willing, but NOT yet — only once it's demonstrably working. Use the pilot *privately* (validation + footage captured now for later); public proof (name/testimonial/case study) waits until proven.
5. **Success targets** — deliberately none yet. Very early: the pilot was just onboarded and their classes aren't set up. The current milestone is **operational, not marketing**: get the pilot's classes live and prove the product works with a real studio.

## 9. Sequencing — the early-stage reality (recommended)

The decisions above say, honestly, that **it is too early for the full video relaunch.** No public pilot proof yet, no paid budget for months, and the pilot isn't even running. Pouring effort into a cinematic relaunch + campaigns now would be building the cathedral before there's anything to prove. Proportionate sequence:

**Now (this week):**
- **Priority #1 — make the pilot succeed.** Set up their classes/schedules/passes, get real bookings, validate the positioning with them privately. This is the actual milestone everything else depends on.
- **Lean landing update, not the cinematic relaunch** — the cheap, high-value subset: add a **booking** section (the missing pillar — real, live, helps recruit *more* founding partners now), fix the review hygiene (canonical, GDPR pixels, sitemap), refresh the hero/CTA to the confirmed studio positioning, and flip the exercise copy at the switchover. No 5-video shoot, no interactive library yet.
- **Start organic B2C content** — post exercise-demo clips (the R2 assets) on TikTok/IG. Free, compounds over the 3–4 months before any paid spend. Start the flywheel early.
- **Capture pilot footage as classes go live** — record now, publish later when they're public.
